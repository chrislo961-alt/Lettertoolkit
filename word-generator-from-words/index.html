    How it works

    Generate words from the letters you already have

    This tool is useful when you have one word, a name or a phrase and want to discover other words hidden inside those letters.

    1

    Enter a source

    Type a single word such as LISTEN, or use a short phrase. Spaces, numbers and punctuation are removed automatically.

    2

    Generate possible words

    Every result is checked against the available letters. No result can use a letter more often than it occurs in your source.

    3

    Start with the longest

    Longest-first sorting makes it easy to spot full anagrams and high-coverage words before browsing shorter options.

    Example

    What words can you make from LISTEN?

    Using the letters in LISTEN, the generator can find six-letter rearrangements such as SILENT as well as shorter words such as LIST, LINE and TILE, depending on the dictionary.

    If your goal is specifically to rearrange every letter, open the Anagram Solver. If you need wildcards, prefix and suffix filters, tile scores or more advanced controls, use the Word Unscrambler.

    Common uses

    When a words-from-words generator helps

    A

    Word games

    Explore playable combinations from a rack or a group of letters, then verify unusual words against the official dictionary for your game.

    B

    Names and phrases

    See which smaller words can be built from a name, title or short phrase for puzzles, classroom exercises and creative prompts.

    C

    Anagram discovery

    Sort longest first to surface words that use most or all of the source letters before looking at shorter combinations.

    How do I generate words from another word?

    Enter the source word or phrase above. The generator searches the LetterToolkit dictionary for words that can be made with those letters.

    Does the generator have to use every letter?

    No. It can return shorter words too. For exact full-letter rearrangements, use the Anagram Solver.

    Can one letter be used twice?

    Only when it appears at least twice in the source. For example, one E cannot be reused to make a result requiring two Es.

    Are all results valid in Scrabble?

    Not necessarily. LetterToolkit uses a broad English word list. Competitive games may use a different official dictionary, so verify rare entries before playing them.
