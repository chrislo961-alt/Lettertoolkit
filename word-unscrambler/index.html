    How to narrow a large result set

    Use the rack first, then add structure

    The best unscrambler workflow is broad-to-specific: enter the rack, inspect the strongest candidates, then add only the constraints you actually know.

    1

    Set useful word lengths

    Use minimum and maximum length when your puzzle or game limits the answer size. This removes noise before you inspect individual words.

    2

    Add known word structure

    Starts with, contains and ends with filters are useful when a crossing clue or visible suffix gives you part of the answer.

    3

    Rank for the task

    Sort longest first for rack coverage, highest tile score for word games, or A–Z when you want predictable browsing.

    Worked example

    Unscrambling LISTEN

    With the letters LISTEN, a full-rack rearrangement is SILENT. The same rack can also form shorter words such as LIST, LINE and TILE, depending on the dictionary used.

    The result message now tells you how many returned candidates use all rack tiles. That makes it easier to separate exact-length anagrams from shorter words without switching tools.

    Dictionary limits

    Why your game may accept a different set of words

    LetterToolkit searches a broad English word list intended for discovery. Commercial word games, school dictionaries and regional dictionaries can include or exclude different spellings, inflections, proper nouns and uncommon words.

    For competitive play, confirm unusual candidates against the official word list for your game. LetterToolkit does not label this broad dataset as an official Scrabble or Words With Friends dictionary.
