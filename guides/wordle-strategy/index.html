Wordle Strategy: How to Use Every Guess Better

A practical guide to narrowing Wordle candidates without relying on a single magic starter word.

Think in information, not just possible answers

Early guesses are valuable when they test common letters and useful vowel combinations. A guess can be strategically good even when it is unlikely to be the final answer.

Treat colors as constraints

Green letters are fixed positions. Yellow letters must appear elsewhere. Gray letters are normally excluded, but repeated letters require care: a gray copy can appear when another copy of the same letter has already satisfied the answer count.

Avoid wasting early guesses

Repeating a confirmed green letter can be necessary later, but early in the puzzle a guess with more untested letters often reveals more information.

Test families of words

If you have _IGHT, many answers may remain. Use a probe guess that tests several possible initial letters rather than guessing one member of the family at a time.

Use the helper transparently

Enter greens in their exact positions, yellows as included letters and grays as exclusions. Then inspect the remaining candidates and choose a guess that distinguishes them.

Keep the game fun

A helper can teach pattern recognition, but using it for every move changes the challenge. Many players prefer to use assistance only when they are stuck.
